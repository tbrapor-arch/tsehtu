# Grammar inventory for Mandarin speakers

What a learner who speaks Mandarin at B2 actually has to learn in Taigi, grouped by topic, with the stage where the
course introduces it. Ranks (`#`) are frequency ranks among MOE dictionary words in the written corpus
(`data/build/taigi.db`, view `v_core`). Hàn-jī follow the MOE dictionary.

**Stages** (provisional, to be fitted to the final pacing):
**A** = first quarter of the course (survival core) · **B** = middle half · **C** = last quarter.

## How the vocabulary splits for this learner
Of the 4,265 core words:
- **2,939 same characters as Mandarin.** Only the reading is new. Taught through sound correspondences
  (see the colloquial/literary note in Lesson 0) and recycled in dialogues, rarely explained.
- **616 Taigi-only words** (佇 tī, 毋 m̄, 攏 lóng, 厝 tshù…). Most grammar words are here. These get the notes.
- **137 false friends**: characters exist in Mandarin but the meaning differs (走 tsáu = run, 緊 kín = fast).
  Flagged every time they appear.
- **543 partial** (家己 ka-kī vs 自己…), easy to guess once seen.

Among the 100 most frequent Taigi words, roughly a third are Taigi-only or false friends. That's where the time goes.

## 3. The negation system · Stage A (core), B (fine points)
The single biggest grammar difference: Mandarin 不 / 沒 / 別 split into six words.

| Taigi | Hàn-jī | Roughly | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| m̄ | 毋 | 不 (will) | refusal, and fixed with sī, tsai, bat, kánn | *M̄ tsai.* "Don't know." |
| bô | 無 | 沒 / 不 | absence; event didn't happen; also neutral "not" before ài, kàu… | *Bô lâng.* |
| bē | 袂 | 不會 / 不能 | inability, impossibility, prediction | *Bē kuânn.* "It's not cold." *Bē-sái.* "Not allowed." |
| buē | 未 | 還沒 | not yet | *Iáu-buē.* |
| mài | 莫 | 別、不要 | imperative "don't" | *Mài khì!* |
| m̄-thang | 毋通 | 不可以、千萬別 | strong prohibition | |

Paired positives: m̄ ↔ (sī/ài), bô ↔ ū, bē ↔ ē, buē ↔ (ū / --ah). Question tags mirror them:
*…--bô?* *…--buē?* *Ē…bē?* Taught as a set in Stage A, revisited in B.

## 10. Verb complements · Stage B
- **V + kah + result** (甲, rank 60): *Thiám kah bē kiânn--ah.* "So tired I can't walk any more." Mandarin 得 / 到.
- **V + ē/bē + result** (會/袂): *khuànn ē tio̍h / khuànn bē tio̍h* = 看得到 / 看不到. Mandarin 得/不 become ē/bē.
- **Result tio̍h** (著, rank 12, false friend): *tshuē-tio̍h* = 找到; *khuànn-tio̍h* = 看到.
- **Directionals** with neutral tone: *--khì, --lâi, --tshut-khì, --khí-lâi, --lo̍h-lâi*; *lo̍h-khì* "go on (doing)".
- **V + māi** (覓): try doing — *khuànn-māi* = 看看.

## 11. Sentence-final particles · Stage A (--ah, --lah, --honnh), B (rest)
--ah 矣 · --lah 啦 · --leh 咧 · --neh 呢 · --honnh 乎 · --ooh 喔 · --mah 嘛 · --looh 囉 · --lih 哩.
All neutral-tone. Taught by ear in dialogues; notes give one function each, not a theory.

## 15. Sound system topics (taught in Lesson 0, then spiralled)
- Tâi-lô spelling vs pinyin (Lesson 0).
- Tone sandhi in tone groups (Lesson 0 intro; then one short note every few lessons). `tools/sandhi.py` generates
  spoken tones from a line with `|` group marks.
- Neutral tone after `--` (Stage A).
- Colloquial vs literary readings (Stage B onward): the same character often has two readings, e.g.
  生 senn / sing, 行 kiânn / hîng, 大 tuā / tāi, 下 ē / hā. Learners with Mandarin can use the literary reading
  as a bridge to guess new words.
- Special sandhi before 仔 á (Stage B).
