# Lesson 0 · Reading Tâi-lô when you already know pinyin

Tâi-lô (台羅) is the Ministry of Education's romanisation, the one used in its dictionary and most modern
materials. It looks a bit like pinyin, and that's the trap: several letters and most tone marks mean something
different. This lesson is the map. You don't need to memorise it now; come back to it.

## 1. Consonants: what changes from pinyin

| Tâi-lô | Sounds like | Pinyin equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **p** | unaspirated p | pinyin **b** | 保 pó |
| **ph** | aspirated p | pinyin **p** | 普 phóo |
| **t** / **th** | unaspirated / aspirated t | pinyin **d** / **t** | 東 tong · 頭 thâu |
| **k** / **kh** | unaspirated / aspirated k | pinyin **g** / **k** | 君 kun · 看 khuànn |
| **ts** / **tsh** | unaspirated / aspirated ts | pinyin **z** / **c** (before i: closer to **j** / **q**) | 水 tsuí · 菜 tshài |
| **b** | a real voiced b, as in English *bed* | none | 米 bí · 無 bô |
| **g** | a real voiced g, as in *go* | none | 五 gōo |
| **j** | voiced dz/z (many speakers say l or g) | none, **not** pinyin j | 日 ji̍t · 字 jī |
| **s** | s (before i a little softer) | pinyin **s** / **x** | 心 sim |
| **h** | h, lighter than pinyin h | pinyin **h** | 好 hó |
| **ng** | ng, also at the start of a syllable | none at the start | 雅 ngá |
| **m, n, l** | as in English | same | 人 lâng |

Gone from Mandarin: no **f**, no **zh ch sh r**, no **x q** as separate letters, no **ü**.

**The two that cost Mandarin speakers the most:** *p t k ts* are the soft ones (pinyin b d g z), and *b g* are
truly voiced, a sound Mandarin doesn't have.

## 2. Vowels and endings

| Tâi-lô | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a, i, u, e | as in pinyin *a, i, u*; **e** is always [e] (like *é*), never the pinyin e [ɤ] | 茶 tê |
| **oo** | open o [ɔ], as in *law* | 古 kóo · 補 póo |
| **o** | closer o [o]~[ə], depending on region | 保 pó · 果 kó |
| **-nn** | the vowel is nasal; there is no n sound | 三 sann · 生 senn · 衫 sann |
| **-m** | lips close at the end (Mandarin lost this) | 心 sim · 甘 kam |
| **-n, -ng** | as in pinyin | 君 kun · 東 tong |
| **-p -t -k** | unreleased stop, cut off short (as in Cantonese) | 十 tsa̍p · 骨 kut · 六 la̍k |
| **-h** | glottal stop: the vowel is cut off | 食 tsia̍h · 欲 beh |
| **m, ng** alone | a syllable with no vowel | 毋 m̄ · 黃 n̂g · 長 tn̂g |

`oo` vs `o` and the nasal `-nn` are the vowels to listen for in the first lessons.

## 3. Tones: the marks lie to pinyin readers

Seven tones. Tones 4 and 8 only occur on syllables ending in -p/-t/-k/-h. (Tone 6 has merged with 7 and isn't used.)

| Tone | Mark | Shape (northern / general) | Pinyin look-alike | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a (none) | high level | like tone 1 ā | — |
| 2 | á | **high falling** | looks like tone 2, sounds like **tone 4** | the acute falls |
| 3 | à | **low falling**, low | looks like tone 4 | low, not high-falling |
| 4 | ah (none) | low-mid, cut short | — | only -p -t -k -h |
| 5 | â | **rising** | sounds like **tone 2** | the circumflex rises |
| 7 | ā | **mid level** | looks like tone 1, but **mid**, not high | lower than tone 1 |
| 8 | a̍h | high, cut short | — | only -p -t -k -h |

Two classic series to train your ear (all real words):

- 君 kun · 滾 kún · 棍 kùn · 骨 kut · 群 kûn · 郡 kūn · 滑 ku̍t
- 東 tong · 黨 tóng · 棟 tòng · 督 tok · 同 tông · 洞 tōng · 毒 to̍k

## 4. Tone sandhi: what you write isn't always what you say

Within a tone group, every syllable except the last changes its tone. The written form is always the citation
tone; the spoken one is predictable:

```
unchecked:  5 → 7 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 7        (south: 5 → 3)
checked:    4 -p/-t/-k → 8     4 -h → 2 (the h disappears)
            8 -p/-t/-k → 3     8 -h → 3 (the h disappears)
```

| Written | Said | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 食飯 tsia̍h-pn̄g | tsià-pn̄g | 8 -h → 3, h dropped; pn̄g is last, unchanged |
| 臺灣人 Tâi-uân-lâng | Tāi-uān-lâng | 5 → 7 twice; lâng is last |
| 你好 Lí hó. | Li hó. | 2 → 1 |
| 法國人 Huat-kok-lâng | Hua̍t-ko̍k-lâng | 4 -t/-k → 8 |
| 你食飽未？ Lí tsia̍h-pá--buē? | Li tsià-pá--buē? | pá is before `--`, so it keeps its tone; buē is neutral |

Where do tone groups end? Roughly at the end of a noun phrase used as subject or topic, at the end of the verb
phrase, and at every pause. The lessons mark the groups for you at first (with `|` in the files, shown as a gap in
the app). By the active phase you'll hear them yourself.

**Neutral tone `--`:** what follows `--` is said light and low, and the syllable before it keeps its full tone.
Common with sentence-final particles (--ah, --lah, --bô) and with objects and directions after a verb.

## 5. Liaison: a final consonant slides into the next syllable

When a syllable ending in a consonant is followed by one that starts with a vowel, usually a light word like
个 ê, 仔 á or a particle (啊 a, 矣 --ah), the final consonant is carried over and becomes the next syllable's
onset. The spelling doesn't change; you hear it, you don't write it.

| Final | Heard as | Written | Said |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-t** | **l** (a light tap, like the Japanese r) | 彼个 hit-ê · 這个 tsit-ê · 一个 tsi̍t ê | hi(t)-lê · tsi(t)-lê · tsi̍(t)-lê |
| **-k** | **g** | 竹仔 tik-á · 角仔 kak-á | tik-gá · kak-gá |
| **-p** | **b** | 盒仔 a̍p-á · 粒仔 lia̍p-á | a̍p-bá · lia̍p-bá |
| **-m / -n / -ng** | **m / n / ng** | 柑仔 kam-á · 囡仔 gín-á · 枋仔 pang-á | kam-má · gín-ná · pang-ngá |

**-h never links:** it's a glottal stop, so 鴨仔 ah-á stays ah-á. Liaison is strongest in relaxed, fast speech
and with 个 and 仔; in careful speech you'll also hear the plain form. Tone sandhi (§4) still applies on top.

## 6. Your Mandarin is a head start: sound correspondences

Most Taigi words share their characters with Mandarin. The readings correspond in regular ways, especially the
literary (文讀) readings:

| Mandarin | Taigi | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| w-, m- | b- | 文 bûn · 萬 bān · 米 bí · 無 bô |
| r-, some y- | j- (or l-) | 日 ji̍t · 人 jîn (literary) / lâng (colloquial) |
| f- | h- (literary), p- (colloquial) | 飛 hui / pue · 分 hun / pun |
| zh, ch, sh | ts, tsh, s, and often t, th (colloquial) | 茶 tê · 長 tn̂g · 竹 tik |
| -n | sometimes -m | 三 sam · 心 sim · 甘 kam |
| syllables that were "entering tone" | end in -p, -t, -k, -h | 十 tsa̍p · 六 la̍k · 骨 kut · 食 tsia̍h |

## 7. One character, two readings

Many characters have a **colloquial** (白讀) reading used in everyday words, and a **literary** (文讀) reading used
in learned words, names and compounds borrowed from writing:

| | colloquial | literary |
|---|---|---|
| 一 | tsi̍t | it |
| 二 / 兩 | nn̄g (兩) | jī (二) |
| 三 | sann | sam |
| 五 | gōo | ngóo |
| 生 | senn | sing |
| 飛 | pue | hui |
| 分 | pun | hun |

Counting things uses the colloquial row (tsi̍t ê, nn̄g ê, sann ê); counting in sequence, dates and phone numbers
often use the literary one. The lessons introduce each reading where it's used and point out the pair.
